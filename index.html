Interactive · monoculture vs. diversity

One crop, one weakness

Release the pest to see how far it spreads.

When a whole field is the same plant, one bug can eat all of it. When lots of different plants grow together, the bug gets stuck.

Uniform fields let one pest or disease run wall to wall. In 1970, Southern corn leaf blight wiped out about 15% of the U.S. corn crop because most hybrids shared the same vulnerable trait. Ullstrup 1972

Simulation: the pest spreads to adjacent plants of the same species only. A teaching model, not an epidemiological one.

Six giants became four

A few very big companies own most of the seeds farmers buy. That means fewer choices.

Between 2015 and 2019, a wave of mergers folded the "Big Six" seed and chemical firms into four. Several of them sell both the herbicide-tolerant seed and the herbicide. IPES-Food

Eat Clean

Real food, step by step

You don't have to be perfect. Start with whole foods and work out from there.

Shop smart

  • Buy whole fruits and vegetables. Very few fresh items in U.S. stores are genetically engineered.
  • Choose USDA Organic for corn, soy, canola, and sugar products. Organic rules ban genetic engineering. USDA NOP
  • Cut back on ultra-processed snacks and drinks.

Where GE crops hide in packaged food:

corn syrupcornstarchsoybean oilsoy lecithincanola oilcottonseed oilsugar (beet)maltodextrin

Find local farms

  • Ask growers how they farm. Most will tell you.
  • USDA Local Food Directories lists farmers markets, CSAs, and on-farm stores.
  • LocalHarvest maps small farms and CSAs.
  • In Kansas City, start at City Market in the River Market.

Save seeds

  • Grow open-pollinated or heirloom varieties. Their seeds grow true to type next year.
  • Hybrid (F1) seeds won't reliably repeat, and patented seeds can't legally be replanted. Bowman v. Monsanto
  • Seed Savers Exchange preserves and shares heirloom seed.

Grow your own

  • Start small: herbs, lettuce, radishes, and bush beans grow fast in pots.
  • Feed the soil with compost. Living soil grows stronger plants.
  • Plant mixed beds instead of single rows. Diversity confuses pests.
